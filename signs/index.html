12Sign shapes and colors, decoded

The short version: In the US, a road sign's outline and background color are a code, set by the federal Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices (MUTCD). An octagon always means stop, a point-down triangle always means yield, yellow means a general warning, orange means a work zone and fluorescent pink means a crash or emergency scene. Street name signs and parking signs follow their own rules about letter size, color and stacking. Most of Europe uses a different grammar under the 1968 Vienna Convention, so a sign you know at home can look unfamiliar abroad.

Did you know?In the 1920s, US stop signs were yellow, because no red material that resisted fading was available.218

What do the different sign shapes mean?

What you see: Signs cut into distinct outlines - octagons, triangles, a sideways pennant, a house-shaped pentagon, a circle, yellow diamonds and plenty of plain rectangles.

The MUTCD says certain shapes "shall be used exclusively for specific signs or series of signs."209 The point is that you can read the sign from its silhouette alone - from behind, at dusk, or with snow stuck to the face. The 2023 11th edition of the federal manual could not be checked directly for this table; the list below comes from Delaware's state MUTCD, which adopts the federal shape rules.209

Shape Reserved for
Octagon Stop
Equilateral triangle, point down Yield
Circle Railroad (grade crossing) advance warning
Pennant (long sideways triangle) No Passing Zone
Pentagon, point up, square bottom corners School advance warning
Pentagon, point up, tapered bottom corners County route markers
Crossbuck (X) The railroad grade crossing itself
Diamond Warning series
Rectangle Regulatory, guide and some warning signs
Trapezoid Recreational and cultural interest areas, national forest routes

Source for the table: Delaware MUTCD Section 2A.09.209

Under the 11th edition, warning signs are generally "diamond-shaped (square with one diagonal vertical)" with a black legend and border on yellow.210 The rectangle is the catch-all, which is why speed limits, street names and freeway guide signs all share it.

What does a sideways triangle sign on the left mean?

What you see: A long yellow triangle pointing to the right, mounted on the left side of a two-lane road, reading NO PASSING ZONE.

This is the pennant, a shape used for exactly one sign: No Passing Zone, code W14-3, in Section 2C.53 of the 11th edition.210 On conventional roads it measures 48 x 48 x 36 inches (about 122 x 122 x 91 cm).210 It sits on the left because that is the side a passing driver is looking at as they pull out.

What does a pentagon-shaped sign mean?

What you see: A house-shaped yellow-green sign with figures of children walking, or a smaller pentagon with a number on it in rural areas.

A point-up pentagon with squared-off bottom corners is reserved for school advance warning; the same outline with tapered bottom corners is a county route marker.209 Under the 11th edition, school and school bus warning signs "shall have a black legend and border on a fluorescent yellow-green background."210

What does a round yellow sign before railroad tracks mean?

What you see: A circular sign on the approach to a level crossing, well before the tracks.

The circle is reserved for grade crossing advance warning.209 It tells you tracks are ahead; the crossbuck - the X-shaped sign - marks the crossing itself.209 The 11th edition also lists related grade crossing advance warning signs with the codes W10-2, W10-3, W10-4, W10-11 and W10-12.210

What do the different sign colors mean?

What you see: Yellow, neon yellow-green, orange, green, blue, brown, red, black and the occasional hot pink sign.

Background color Meaning in the US

Black One-way, night speed limit and truck regulatory signs211
Yellow Warning signs, except temporary ones211
Fluorescent yellow-green Pedestrian, bicycle and school warnings211
Orange Temporary traffic control (work zones)211
Fluorescent pink Incident management (crashes, emergencies)211

The color table comes from trafficsign.us, a long-running reference that tracks the MUTCD, rather than from the manual's own table, which could not be loaded.211

Why are some school and crosswalk signs neon green?

What you see: A glowing yellow-green sign with a walking figure, a bicycle or two children, much brighter than the yellow signs around it.

That color is fluorescent yellow-green. The 11th edition makes it mandatory for school and school bus warning signs; for pedestrian, bicycle and playground warnings, agencies "may" use either yellow or fluorescent yellow-green.210 So one town's crosswalk sign can be plain yellow and the next town's neon, and both are correct.

What does a pink road sign mean?

What you see: A hot pink or magenta sign, often on a portable stand, reading something like ACCIDENT AHEAD or DETOUR.

Fluorescent pink is the background for incident management signs - temporary signs put out at crashes and other emergencies.211 Think of it as the short-notice cousin of orange: orange is for planned work zones, pink is for the unplanned.211

What do sign codes like W14-3 mean?

What you see: Nothing on the sign face. The codes show up on work orders, sign shop labels and in the manual itself.

Every standard US sign has an alphanumeric designation, explained further in Fonts, stickers and numbers on signs. The 11th edition's warning chapter is full of them: W1-8 Chevron Alignment, W7-1 Hill, W2-1 Cross Road, W3-5 Reduced Speed Limit Ahead, W14-3 No Passing Zone.210 A trailing "P" marks a plaque, a small supplementary sign mounted under another one, such as the W13-1P Advisory Speed plaque.210 Mile markers are the D10-1 series.212 FHWA publishes the full drawings for each code in its book "Standard Highway Signs," with a 2024 edition linked from the MUTCD site.213

The two most familiar codes are R1-1 for the Stop sign and R1-2 for the Yield sign, both listed in the 11th edition's table of reserved shapes in Section 2A.05.214 The street name sign is D3-1, detailed in Section 2D.45.214

The leading letters name the sign family. Minnesota DOT's Standard Signs and Markings manual, which it is revising in 2026 to follow the state's 11th-edition MUTCD, files signs under R (regulatory), W (warning), D (guide), I (informational), E (exit), M (route signs, detour signs and auxiliary plaques), S (school), OM (object markers), G (construction) and PM (pavement messages), plus an X section for miscellaneous signs.215 That is Minnesota's arrangement; other states publish their own sign books.

Other countries keep their own catalogs. Australia's kangaroo sign is W5-29, for example.216

Why are stop signs octagons, and were they ever yellow?

What you see: A red octagon with a white border and the word STOP, in the US normally about 30 inches (76 cm) across.

The stop sign was born in Detroit. Signalfan.com puts the "first recorded stop sign" there in 1915, black letters on white metal;217 Wikipedia credits police sergeant Harry Jackson with a 1914 sign and says Michigan adopted stop signs statewide in 1915.218 The sources disagree on the year by one.

In 1922 a national committee backed by AASHO (today's AASHTO) chose the octagon, partly so a driver facing the back of the sign could still recognize it.218 A 1924 meeting set the background to yellow,217 because no red material that resisted fading was available.218 The first MUTCD in 1935 specified a 24 x 24 inch octagon with red or black letters on yellow.217 The 1948 manual required all regulatory and warning signs to be reflectorized.217 In 1954 the background changed to red with white lettering,217 which matched the red of the traffic signal and gave "stop" one color everywhere.218 (For the signal side of that story, see How a City Works: traffic lights.)

Today's standard US stop sign is 30 inches with a 10 inch legend, 35 inches on multilane expressways, 45 inches in oversize versions, and 24 inches at minimum.218 To count as visible at night, its white must measure at least 35 and its red at least 7 on FHWA's retroreflectivity scale, with a contrast ratio of at least 3:1.219 Minnesota's guidance on the 11th edition adds that a stop sign may not be fully covered by a differently shaped sign mounted on its back - the octagon has to stay readable from behind.220

What are the rules for street name signs?

What you see: A rectangle at the corner, usually white letters on green, the name in capital-and-lowercase, mounted above a stop sign or hung over the intersection on a mast arm.

Street name signs follow federal rules on letter size by road type. A summary of the 2009 MUTCD (Section 2D.43 in that edition's numbering) gives these minimums:221

Road Capitals / lowercase
Local two-lane roads, 25 mph or less (allowed) 4 in / 3 in (10 / 7.6 cm)
Two-lane roads, and multilane roads 40 mph or less 6 in / 4.5 in (15 / 11.4 cm)
Multilane roads over 40 mph 8 in / 6 in (20 / 15 cm)
Overhead signs (suggested) 12 in / 9 in (30 / 23 cm)

The names must be in mixed case - an initial capital, the rest lowercase - and the sign must be retroreflective or lit.221 In a test on the then-unopened Capital Beltway, drivers preferred mixed-case lettering to all capitals.222

Four color schemes are allowed: white letters on green, blue or brown, or black letters on white.221 Some cities have gone their own way anyway: Mesa, Arizona used ruby red, and San Jose used white on black until the 2010s.223 Minneapolis uses blue for snow emergency routes and green elsewhere.223 FHWA eliminated the deadline for replacing street name signs to meet retroreflectivity minimums, though the signs are still expected to meet those minimums under the agency's maintenance method.219

The history goes back further than the MUTCD: a 1916 Iowa State College bulletin found that "by far the greater portion" of common enameled-iron street name signs were already made in white on blue.224

Why are some street signs blue instead of green?

What you see: A blue street name sign where the rest of the neighborhood uses green.

Blue is one of the four permitted color schemes, so a town can pick it for every sign.221 Cities also use color to carry a second meaning, as Minneapolis does with blue snow emergency routes.223 SlashGear, a tech site, says blue signs often mark privately maintained roads such as those in gated communities or homeowners' associations;225 treat that as a common local habit, not a national rule.

Why are there so many parking signs on one pole?

What you see: A pole with five to eight small signs stacked vertically - street cleaning, permit parking, two-hour limit, tow zone, loading zone - that seem to contradict each other.

In US cities each parking restriction usually gets its own sign, so one curb space can end up with seven or eight signs stacked several feet high.226 Green lettering is used on permissive parking signs,211 and red on prohibitions such as NO PARKING ANY TIME.227 Reading a stack means working through every sign and finding the ones that apply at this hour on this day. The curbs section covers painted curb colors, which some places use alongside or instead of parking signs.

Brooklyn designer Nikki Sylianteng, after a $95 ticket, drew a grid alternative: one column per day of the week, green blocks for when parking is allowed, red for when it is not, black for loading hours.226 Los Angeles installed 100 of her signs downtown in 2015 for a six-month trial.226

In the federal manual, parking signs make up the R7 series. The 2003 edition's figure of No Parking signs runs from R7-1 NO PARKING ANY TIME through R7-8 reserved accessible parking to plaques such as R7-201 TOW-AWAY ZONE.227

In Boston

Boston piloted LED-lit street name signs at three Downtown Crossing locations in winter 2012; they were about 35 percent larger than the signs they replaced.228 BTD's 2016 sign guide sizes pole-mounted street name signs at 9 in (23 cm) tall under its spec A51, and mast-arm signs at 18 in (46 cm) tall and up to 72 in (183 cm) long under spec A50; the guide does not give colors or letter heights.229 Next door, Brookline kept raised-letter signs with black letters on silver, an exception to the green-and-white standard.230 In late 2005 Boston posted more than 100 blue-and-white "Evacuation Route" signs231 - blue being the MUTCD color for evacuation routes.211

Elsewhere in the world

Most of Europe and many other countries follow the 1968 Vienna Convention, which uses a different shape grammar: red-bordered triangles for danger warnings, red-ringed circles for prohibitions, blue circles for mandatory instructions and rectangles for information.232 The convention allows diamonds for warnings,232 but the yellow diamond is mainly a habit of North America, Australia and Japan.

The Vienna stop sign is the same red octagon with white STOP, in English or the national language; a circle-with-triangle variant was dropped in a 2025 amendment.218 Japan uses a red inverted triangle, Israel a raised-hand pictogram, and Bangladesh and Nepal omit the text.218

Guide sign colors swap around too. Japan uses green on expressways and blue on ordinary roads.233 In the UK, many street name plates use David Kindersley's MoT Serif lettering, which was rejected for road signs.223 The fonts, codes and international systems are covered in Fonts, stickers and numbers on signs. For how roads and their rules are governed, see How a City Works: roads.

Quick answers

Why are stop signs octagons?

A national committee chose the octagon in 1922 so drivers could recognize a stop sign by shape alone, even from behind. The US MUTCD reserves the octagon exclusively for stop signs.

Were stop signs ever yellow?

Yes. From 1924 until 1954 US stop signs had a yellow background because no fade-resistant red material was available. The 1954 MUTCD switched them to white on red to match traffic signals.

What does a pink road sign mean?

Fluorescent pink is the US background color for incident management signs, the temporary signs used at crashes and other emergencies. Orange is used for planned work zones.

Why are some school signs neon green?

Under the MUTCD 11th edition, school and school bus warning signs must use a black legend on fluorescent yellow-green. Pedestrian, bicycle and playground warnings may use either yellow or fluorescent yellow-green.
